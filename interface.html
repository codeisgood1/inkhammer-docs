Using Ink Hammer

The main window

The map fills the window and the panels float around it. Click a number, or use the arrow keys, to step through every part.

  1. MenusFile (new, open, save, export, package, settings), Edit (undo, copy, paste, copy settings), View (fit, grid, size references, fast editing), Window (show or hide panels), Tools (Claude connection, Asset Manager, prefab builder, specialized tools), Plugins and Help.
  2. Map name and scenesThe open map, then its scenes. Scenes are versions of the same map (day and night, before and after the battle). Click + to add one.
  3. Tool railThe tools, top to bottom in the order you usually build a map: Select, Paint, Pen, Height, Place, Build, Roofs, Lights, Walls, Labels, Grid. Hover a tool for its name and shortcut. See The tools.
  4. Tool optionsSettings for the tool you are using. The ? button on its title shows how-to tips for that tool.
  5. The mapDrag with the middle mouse button (or hold Space and drag) to pan, turn the wheel to zoom, press F1 to fit the whole map. The + on each edge makes or opens the map next door.
  6. View switchesGrid and Snap on or off, Walls view (shows the walls, doors and windows a virtual tabletop will get), Labels (show or hide all labels), and Book view (the map in grayscale, as it prints in a black and white book; sliders for brightness, contrast and midtones, and Page size to zoom to about print size. Export > Book saves that look).
  7. Speed switchesSun shadows, lamp shadows, contact shade (AO), animation, draft detail and the void, then the render scale. Turning these off makes the editor faster. They only change the editor: exports always have everything. See Speed switches.
  8. LevelsFloors above and below this one (a cellar, an upper storey). The level below shows through where this one has no ground.
  9. LayersEverything on the map sits on a layer. Drag to reorder, click the eye to hide, the lock to lock. Right-click for more. See Layers.
  10. PropertiesSettings for what is selected: an object, a layer, a light, a wall. With nothing selected it shows the current layer.
  11. AssetsThe art library. Pick a folder, search by name or tag, then click a picture and click the map to place it. The Prefabs tab holds ready-made groups (a loaded cart, a market stall).
  12. Status barHints for the tool you are using on the left. On the right: speed (frames per second), counts of objects and lights, the current layer and the zoom.

Panels

Panels dock like Photoshop's: along the left, along the right and along the bottom, stacked, or floating over the map.

  • Move a panel: press on any empty part of its title bar and drag. Near the left or right column or the bottom, a blue box shows where it will go and the panels there make room when you let go. Let go over the map and it floats.
  • Resize: edges light up orange under the mouse. Drag the line between two docked panels to share the room differently, a column's edge to make it wider or narrower, the top of the bottom panels to make them taller.
  • A panel's own height: drag the outer top or bottom line of a docked panel (one with no other panel against it) and it keeps that height, held at the other edge: drag the 3D view's top line down and it shrinks to the bottom of its column. Double-click that line to let it share the column again.
  • A panel at the bottom of the left column, below the tool buttons, fills the corner under them.
  • Tool buttons: drag them by the small handle at the top; drag them to the left edge to put them back.
  • Floating panels resize from every edge and corner. Double-click a floating panel's title to put it back in its dock.
  • Resizing never docks a panel; only dragging its title bar does.
  • The arrow on a title folds the panel up to its title bar.
  • Lost a panel, or a layout in a tangle? Window lists every panel (tick one to bring it back), and Window > Reset layout puts them all back.

Levels

  • + Level above and + Level below (top right of the map) make a floor above or below this one, each its own map in the same folder. The set button next to the file name (Upper floor (3 maps)) opens any of them.
  • Wherever a level has no ground painted (or a hole in a building's floor), the level below shows through, lined up with it at 100% scale. Level look... sets its blur, haze, scale and shift, or a Background image of your own (a sea, clouds, a city far below) instead.
  • The picture of a level is taken when you save it or leave it. After changing a lower level, save it so the levels above show the change.
  • A violet fill where the level below should be means there is no picture of it yet (open it and save once) or its file has moved; the status bar says which.
  • File > Save As on a level offers to copy the whole set into the new folder, so the links keep working.

Selecting and changing things

With the Select tool (V):

  • Click to select, drag to move. Drag a corner to scale, drag just outside a corner to turn.
  • Drag on an empty part of the map to draw a box round several things. Shift+click adds or removes one. Alt+click picks the next thing in a stack.
  • Ctrl+drag drags out a copy. Ctrl+D duplicates.
  • R and Shift+R turn 15 degrees, F mirrors, [ and ] scale, arrow keys nudge.
  • Right-click anything for its menu: tint, flip, turn 90 degrees, bring to front, move to a layer, group into a layer, add a mask, link, make a prefab, distort, hide, lock, delete. Buildings and roofs have one too, with Properties... and Edit walls, rooms and doors.
  • The number box beside a slider takes the value in the units it shows: type 3 in a door width shown in feet and press Enter for a 3 ft door.
  • With two or more things picked, the align bar over the map lines them up in a row or column, or spaces them evenly. It works on the points of a line you are editing and on Build-tool corners too.
  • Anything past the edge of the map (a prop hanging over it, a side map) sits under a grey veil, so you can see where the map ends. You can still click and edit it; exports are not affected.
Copy a look

Ctrl+Shift+C copies how one thing looks; Ctrl+Shift+V pastes that look onto others of the same kind.
